1. Most important new findings

  1. Immune dysfunction is now supported by the largest CFC-specific cohort to date. In 56 molecularly confirmed individuals, 32% had increased infection susceptibility, 25% autoimmune manifestations, and substantial rates of lymphopenia and low immunoglobulins. The study also suggests gene-specific patterns: BRAF variants with T-cell lymphopenia, and MAP2K1 variants with monocytosis, reduced naïve/memory B cells, and hypogammaglobulinemia. Routine immunologic assessment should therefore be considered a clinically actionable CFC-care issue. (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)

  2. Cancer-risk evidence has become more nuanced. The CFC pooled analysis of 690 reported individuals found elevated overall cancer and acute lymphoblastic leukemia standardized-incidence ratios, with risk apparently concentrated in early childhood; however, ascertainment limitations and only 11 cancers mean the estimates remain imprecise. Conversely, a genomic-ascertainment study found no sufficient evidence of increased cancer prevalence among adult CFC-heterozygotes, supporting age-stratified—not generalized lifelong—risk interpretation. (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)

  3. Neurobehavioral needs are more clearly defined. A caregiver cohort of 61 people with CFC found challenging behavior in 77%, most prominent in late childhood/adolescence and associated with sleep disturbance, pain interference, sensory differences, epilepsy, and adaptive-function level. (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)

  4. CFC neurobiology has gained two complementary BRAF-model studies. In BRAF-mutant mice and human cortical spheroids, ERK hyperactivation in astrocytes was linked to calcium dysregulation, reactive astrogliosis, and memory impairment; astrocytic ERK or calcium modulation rescued phenotypes in mice. A separate CFC BrafQ241R model indicates that mature-neuron MAPK dysregulation can produce learning/activity abnormalities even without developmental malformations. (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)

  5. The update expands practical phenotype surveillance across dermatology, feeding/GI, behavior, and rare tumor-like presentations—including MAP2K1-associated skin pathology, granulomatous miliaria profunda, MAP2K2/CFC4 feeding and neurodevelopmental disease, and reported Langerhans cell histiocytosis in two CFC individuals. These are mostly case-level signals, not established associations. (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)

4. Recommended next-update search expansion

Use the CFC umbrella terms plus gene-specific strings:

("cardiofaciocutaneous syndrome" OR "cardio-facio-cutaneous syndrome" OR CFCS OR CFC)

 (CFC OR "cardiofaciocutaneous"))

Add newly high-yield clinical concept blocks

  • Immunology: hypogammaglobulinemia, immunodeficiency, inborn errors of immunity, vaccine response, lymphopenia, B cell, T cell, autoimmunity, recurrent infection.
  • Cancer/hematology: malignancy, cancer predisposition, acute lymphoblastic leukemia, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, hepatoblastoma, juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia, clonal hematopoiesis.
  • Neurodevelopment/behavior: self-injury, aggression, challenging behavior, autism, sensory processing, sleep disturbance, pain, epilepsy, white matter, astrocyte.
  • Feeding/GI/growth: feeding dysfunction, oropharyngeal dysphagia, failure to thrive, growth velocity, short stature, gastrostomy, gastrointestinal malformation, intestinal duplication.
  • Dermatology: miliaria profunda, eccrine, dermatopathology, keratosis pilaris, ulerythema ophryogenes, alopecia, hemangioma, dermoid cyst.
  • Models/therapeutics: iPSC, cortical spheroid, organoid, BRAF Q241R, BRAF K499E, MEK inhibition, ERK inhibition, RAS/MAPK modulation.

Adjacent concepts to monitor, but keep separate from core CFC evidence

  1. CFC-like chromosomal disorders and diagnostic mimics—especially deletions and ectodermal dysplasia syndromes. Maintain a separate Differential diagnosis / phenotype mimics tag rather than mixing them with molecularly confirmed CFC.
  2. Gene-specific CFC subtypes—search and tag BRAF-CFC, MAP2K1-CFC, MAP2K2-CFC/CFC4, and KRAS-CFC independently, because the immunology and phenotype literature increasingly reports gene-associated differences. (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)
  3. Age-stratified cancer surveillance—separate pediatric from adult evidence in future screening, because recent CFC cancer studies reach different but potentially compatible conclusions depending on ascertainment and age. (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)
